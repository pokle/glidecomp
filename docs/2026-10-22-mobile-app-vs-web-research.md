# Web wins arrivals; the app wins landings

*Research as of 22 October 2026. The app's build status was updated on 24 October 2026, after mobile stages 6–10 merged ([#758](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/pull/758)–[#762](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/pull/762)).*

*Outcome: the owner decided to remove the native app and put the effort into the website. The `mobile/` workspace and the backend only it used were deleted after this report. Links into them below point at the last commit that had them.*

Taken as a whole, GlideComp's users would not find a native app *much* easier than the website, and first-time users would find it harder. The app's advantage is real but narrow. It falls on a few repeated moments in the field: handing in a track from a landing paddock on one bar of signal, rereading today's task with no signal at all, and being told that scores have moved. The advantage is largest on iPhones. As of late 2026, Safari still cannot put a site in the share sheet, sync in the background or finish an upload after the page closes, and it offers push only after a manual Add to Home Screen. Everyone who arrives from a Google result or a WhatsApp, Telegram or Facebook post is better served by the server-rendered page: spectators, family, and a pilot at their first comp. A forced install loses most of those visitors (**69% abandoned Google's full-page "get the app" interstitial outright**), and Universal Links rarely fire inside chat apps' built-in browsers. The "repeat" pilot also repeats less than the word suggests. **The average ranked pilot enters about 1.6 WPRS comps a year, each a median five days with three valid tasks.** Most pilots are therefore daily users for a week and strangers for months, which is the pattern in which typical apps keep only 3–6% of users at Day 30. The truly frequent user is the organiser, and the app's organiser tools only merged on 24 October. None of this has been tested on GlideComp's own users. The repo holds no analytics or user research, and the first real contact will be the stage 10 beta, which is waiting on the store accounts. The clearest interface lesson of 2025–26 is that legibility beat spectacle. Apple spent a year walking Liquid Glass back, and Google's own research found that dropping familiar lists and labels hurt usability. Both points matter twice over for screens read in Australian summer glare.

## Every first visit ends on a web page, whatever ships to the stores

In free flight, people meet a competition through a link. **PWCA rules require "official communication groups" for every event**, and the two-hour complaint window runs from when provisional results are posted there ([PWCA Rules 2025](https://pwca.org/storage/4468/PWCA.RULES.2025.FINAL.V2025-01.pdf)). The Swiss Open 2026 named Telegram "The primary information channel for the competition" ([Swiss Open 2026 briefing](https://www.swissleague.ch/files/v260618230442/upload/SwissOpen2026_GeneralBriefing_V1.pdf)). Local coverage of the Corryong Cup sends readers to a Facebook page ([Corryong Courier](https://arr.news/2026/01/22/cup-draws-pilots-from-across-australia-and-overseas/)). GlideComp's information architecture is judged by how fast its jobs get done "starting from a Google result or a bookmarked comp URL" ([IA v2](2026-07-08-information-architecture-v2.md)). The public comp pages are server-rendered so that crawlers and link-preview bots see text ([SSR](ssr.md)). Since [#535](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/issues/535), `/submit` is on every page and works with no account. It also takes `?comp=` and `?task=`, so the URL "is worth putting on a poster or behind a QR code at launch" ([track submission](track-submission.md)). For a first visit, the website already does the one thing an app cannot: tap a link and see the page.

The evidence on install friction is old, but nothing has contradicted it. In 2014 Google tested a full-page app interstitial on Google+, and **69% of visits abandoned the page**: those visitors neither went to the store nor continued to the site. Replacing the interstitial with a small banner **raised mobile-web 1-day active users by 17%**, while app installs moved only −2% ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2015/07/google-case-study-on-app-download-interstitials)). Google's current guidance, updated in December 2025, says intrusive dialogs "may lead to poor search performance" and names app install prompts explicitly ([Google: avoid intrusive interstitials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/avoid-intrusive-interstitials)). An install push on a public comp page would therefore cost search ranking as well as visitors. NN/g's long-standing finding is that people "will install an app only if they expect to use it often" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-native-apps/)). Someone following a friend's results link does not expect that.

The ways of bridging a link into an app have narrowed. **Android Instant Apps stopped working in December 2025** ([Google Play services reference](https://developers.google.com/android/reference/com/google/android/gms/instantapps/package-summary)). **Firebase Dynamic Links, the free deferred-deep-link service, shut down on 25 August 2025** ([Linkrunner, vendor](https://linkrunner.io/blog/firebase-dynamic-links-migration-guide-2026-what-to-do-now-that-it-s-shut-down)). iOS App Clips survive, but an App Clip cannot run background activity (background URL sessions included), cannot reach Files, and anything it does the full app must also do ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/appclip/choosing-the-right-functionality-for-your-app-clip.md)). That is the wrong shape for a track upload. Universal Links, merged in stage 9, help only people who already have the app. The in-app browsers of Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, where comp links travel, generally keep navigation inside their own webview rather than hand it to the app ([Linkrunner, vendor](https://linkrunner.io/blog/universal-links-app-links-break-in-app-browsers)). Handoff's production team found that pushing Facebook-browser users out to the default browser actually *reduced* conversion ([Handoff](https://handoff.ai/tech/shipping-a-universal-expo-app-to-web-ios-and-android-in-production-lessons-from-handoff)). As a rough funnel, suppose a store page converts somewhere between AppTweak's organic App Store average of **8.6%** ([AppTweak, vendor](https://www.apptweak.com/aso-blog/mobile-app-store-conversion-rate-benchmarks-per-category)) and perhaps 50% for a motivated visitor, then lose 10–20% between install and first open and some deep-link context on top. Only about 5–40% of link-clickers would then see the content inside the app. That is an estimate built from vendor-grade rates, not a measurement, but even its generous end loses most first-time visitors.

Free flight's own history points the same way. The PWCA launched a €2.99 Android spectator app in 2017, and there is no later trace of it ([XC Mag](https://xcmag.com/news/pwca-live-app-paragliding-world-cup-action-on-your-phone/)). In 2025 the Red Bull X-Alps official tracker was described as "so bad that most viewers are looking for alternative platforms". Fans moved to PureTrack's lighter *web* page, not to an app ([Hyperpilot](https://hyperknot.com/hyperpilot/how-to-set-up-puretrack-for-x-alps/)). Spectators judge a page by how fast it loads and how it works on a phone, not by how it was built. Yet the mobile plan contains no web-to-app promotion at all ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)). When the app ships, Apple's Smart App Banner is the sanctioned route. It is a dismissable top banner that does not reappear after it is closed. It opens the app if the app is installed, and its `app-argument` can carry the current URL so the reader carries on where they were ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/webkit/promoting-apps-with-smart-app-banners.md)). On the Google+ evidence, a banner is close to free and an interstitial is not.

## A comp pilot is a daily user for a week, then a stranger for months

The population is small and works in bursts. CIVL ranks **6,979 paragliding XC pilots and 732 hang gliding Class 1 pilots** as of October 2026 ([CIVL PG XC](https://civlcomps.org/ranking/paragliding-xc/pilots); [CIVL HG Class 1](https://civlcomps.org/ranking/hang-gliding-class-1-xc/pilots)). The paragliding ranking table holds **156 comps starting in 2024 and 164 in 2025**. They run a median five days, with a median of three valid tasks and 67 pilots. Hang gliding has about 40 ranked comps a year with a median field of 23.5 ([CIVL PG XC comps](https://civlcomps.org/ranking/paragliding-xc/competitions?ranking_id=1946); [CIVL HG comps](https://civlcomps.org/ranking/hang-gliding-class-1-xc/competitions)). Dividing the table's 33,607 pilot-entries by the ranked pilots gives about **1.6 ranked comps per pilot per year**. That average hides a skew: circuit pilots fly far more, and most pilots fly one or two. In Australia, the highcloud AirScore host carries 145 race comps since 2020, about 22 a year ([AirScore import plan](2026-07-21-airscore-history-import-plan.md)), clustered in the December–March summer.

That rhythm is hard on apps. Typical **Day-30 retention is 3–6%** in most categories, and even news, the best category, reaches only 15.3% on iOS ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-app-retention)). In 2021, US users opened about 46 of their roughly 80 installed apps in a month ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-us-users-are-using-46)). The famous app-beats-web figures, such as comScore's "18x more time" in apps against web audiences "almost 3 times bigger" ([MediaPost on comScore](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/284713/why-mobile-web-is-outpacing-app-ecosystem.html)), mostly show who chooses to install. They are a selection effect, not proof that an app causes the lift. One figure is encouraging: sports apps had the lowest 30-day uninstall rate of any US category, at about 38% ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1278927/uninstall-rate-us-app-categories-by-install)), so a pilot's GlideComp app may still be on the phone at the next comp. Installed is not the same as ready, though. Months later the app may need a store update or a fresh sign-in, which feels more like a first visit than a return. EAS Update closes much of that gap for JavaScript-only changes ([Handoff](https://handoff.ai/tech/shipping-a-universal-expo-app-to-web-ios-and-android-in-production-lessons-from-handoff)). The website has its own weakness across that gap. Its anonymous-submit memory lives in `localStorage` ([track submission](track-submission.md)), and on an iPhone Safari's tracking prevention deletes script-written storage after seven days of browsing without a visit to the site ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/what-safaris-7-day-cap-on-script-writeable-storage-means-for-pwa-developers-332519)). By inference, an iPhone pilot coming back months later starts that form from scratch. The native app keeps the same identifier in its secure store ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)).

Within the comp week, the app is clearly easier at the most stressful moment. XC Mag warns first-timers that "1 in 30 tracklogs can be mis-scored" and that pilots "usually have two hours to check" ([XC Mag](https://xcmag.com/fly-better/paragliding-techniques-paramotoring-skills/comp-series-first-tasks-what-to-expect/)). The Swiss Open set its backup-track deadline at "no later than 1 hour after first publication of provisional results" ([Swiss Open 2026](https://www.swissleague.ch/files/v260618230442/upload/SwissOpen2026_GeneralBriefing_V1.pdf)). The file is often not where the pilot is. Australia's HG Comps says "Most apps store the tracklog on the app's server, not on your phone" ([HG Comps](https://hgcomps.com.au/?catid=15&id=13%3Ausing-your-phone-to-record-a-tracklog)), and XCTrack's tutorial notes that chat apps will not carry a `.xctsk` directly ([XCTrack](https://xctrack.org/Competition_Tutorial.html)). Organisers already take backup tracks by Telegram and email, which amounts to a share sheet with a person in the loop. Stage 5 of the app, merged on 17 October ([#740](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/pull/740)), answers this directly. IGC is registered as a document type on iOS, with VIEW and SEND intents on Android, so "Open in GlideComp" lands in the submit sheet ([app config](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/mobile/app.config.ts)). Every track goes through a queue. On iOS a background URL session "sends it when there is one, even with the app suspended", and the sheet says plainly "No signal, so your track is saved on this phone. It hasn't been submitted yet" ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md); [submit sheet](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/mobile/src/app/submit.tsx)). Apple's background sessions continue while the app is suspended and relaunch it if it was terminated ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/foundation/downloading-files-in-the-background.md)). The website remembers the comp, task and identifier ("Day two of a six-day comp then costs no typing", [track submission](track-submission.md)) and retries transient failures. But it has no offline cache and no queue: with no signal, it has nothing to show and nowhere to save the track.

| Journey | Who, how often | Website today | Native app today | Easier on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Follow results from a shared link | Spectator, family; once or occasionally | SSR page, no install, no sign-in | Needs an install; Universal Links (stage 9) help only those who have it | **Web** |
| First track at a first comp | Pilot; once | `/submit` anonymous, QR or poster prefill | Install first, then submit | **Web** |
| Daily track after landing, weak signal | Pilot; 3–5 times a comp | File picker; fails offline; no iOS share target | Share-sheet import, offline queue, background upload (built) | **App**, clearly on iOS |
| Reread today's task with no signal | Pilot; daily | Nothing cached | Persisted cache with a "No signal" banner (built) | **App** |
| Hear that scores moved | Pilot; daily | Submission email only | Opt-in push per comp, including "scores recomputed" (stage 9, merged) | **App** |
| Run a comp day from the hill | Organiser; daily in a comp | Mobile-first reworked admin pages | Stage 7 (merged): Pilots & tracks, route editor, roster, settings; reads from the cache and writes when there is signal | **Untested**; quick entry and file import stay on the web |
| Set up a comp and GAP parameters | Organiser; once per comp | Desk work on the website | Possible since stage 7 (create a comp; Scoring and GAP settings) | **Web**, at a desk |

The pattern is consistent. The web wins every row a person meets once. The app wins the rows a pilot meets daily in the field. The organiser rows are still open: stage 7 merged on 24 October, and nobody has run a comp day with it yet ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)). Platform mix matters too: XCTrack is Android-only ([XContest](https://www.xcontest.org/xctrack/)) and FlySkyHy iPhone-only ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flyskyhy/id516879039)), and the repo has no device or iOS/Android split. GlideComp therefore cannot yet say how many of its pilots sit on the side where the app's advantage is largest.

## The remaining gap is an iPhone capability gap, not a layout gap

The website has already absorbed most of what used to make apps feel easier on a phone. Since the "Mobile first" change of stance on 2026-09-19, it has gained a **44 px touch floor** on coarse pointers and an edge-to-edge layout with safe-area insets. The waypoints page was rebuilt from a 145-row grid that scrolled sideways into a list of full-screen sheets, and its locate target grew from 28x28 to 48x44 px. Sheets now close with Back. Admin editors moved into routed pages, because "a form taller than the viewport inside a centred modal is the desktop-most thing an app can do". Drag-to-reorder gave way to up/down buttons, because HTML5 drag "never fires on touch without a long-press" ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md); [IA v2](2026-07-08-information-architecture-v2.md); [RAC guide](2026-07-18-rac-adoption-guide.md); [accessibility standard](accessibility-standard.md)). The native IA itself says the waypoints page was "already reworked on the web for exactly this shape, so the native app inherits a settled design", and its Submit tab copies the web's #535 ([iOS IA](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-09-22-ios-app-information-architecture.md)). Both clients share `@glidecomp/engine` and `@glidecomp/client`, and a test fails if either one grows its own report-card derivation ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)). The screens are converging, so any case for "much easier" has to rest on what each platform lets the code do.

| Field capability | Chrome on Android | Safari on iOS 27 | GlideComp website | GlideComp app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Appear in the share sheet for `.igc` | Yes (76+) | **No** | Yes, but lands on the account-only analysis page | Yes (built) |
| Finish an upload after the page or app closes | Background Sync 49+, Background Fetch 74+ | **No** | Not used | iOS background session (built) |
| Push notifications | Yes (42+) | Only after a manual Add to Home Screen (16.4+) | Not used | Opt-in per comp (stage 9) |
| Programmatic install prompt | Yes (44+) | **No** | Not used | Not applicable |
| Offline reading | Service worker cache | Service worker cache, best-effort storage | "no offline caching" | Persisted cache (built) |

The browser columns come from MDN's compatibility data, version 8.1.7, 2026-10-22 ([BCD](https://unpkg.com/@mdn/browser-compat-data/data.json); [Apple forums on iOS web push](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/732594)). The GlideComp columns come from the [manifest](../web/frontend/public/manifest.webmanifest), the [service worker](../web/frontend/public/sw.js) and the [public deep-link gate](../web/frontend/src/analysis/public-deep-link.ts).

iOS 26 lowered the bar to install a web app: "By default, every website added to the Home Screen opens as a web app" ([WebKit, WWDC25](https://webkit.org/?p=16993)). But there is still no install prompt. In 2024, when Apple tried to remove home-screen web apps in the EU, it cited "very low user adoption" of them ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/03/01/apple-home-screen-web-apps-ios-17-eu/)). The Safari 27 beta added only the Service Worker static routing API for web apps ([WebKit, WWDC26](https://webkit.org/?p=17967)). Storage stays best-effort and is evicted least-recently-used first, unless the browser grants `persist()` ([WebKit storage policy](https://webkit.org/?p=14403)). The regulators keep pushing: the EU's DMA, the UK's Strategic Market Status designation of Apple in October 2025, and Japan's smartphone act from December 2025. Even so, no browser has shipped a non-WebKit engine on iOS anywhere ([Open Web Advocacy](https://open-web-advocacy.org/blog/owa-2025-review/)). **On iOS, the native app is the only route to share-sheet import, background upload and dependable push. On Android, an installed Chrome web app has nearly all three.**

GlideComp's own web app uses less than Android allows. The manifest opens at `/analysis.html`. The share target hands an IGC to the analysis page, which sends an anonymous visitor off to sign in. The service worker says outright that "All other requests fall through to the network (no offline caching)" ([sw.js](../web/frontend/public/sw.js)). An Android pilot who shares a track from XCTrack to the installed GlideComp web app today reaches the personal track viewer, not the comp's submit form. By inference, pointing `share_target` and `start_url` at `/submit`, and caching the comp the pilot last opened, would give Android web users much of stage 5 without an install. It would also reach first-comp pilots the app never will. The native IA's list of "What a native app owes that the web cannot" is a list of claims; nobody has measured it against an upgraded web app ([iOS IA](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-09-22-ios-app-information-architecture.md)).

The organiser is the case where an install pays back best, yet the app's organiser tools are its newest and least proven. Across the sport, organisers use desktop and web tools. FS, the FAI/CIVL scorer, is Windows-only; its 2026 R1.3 release came out on 20 July 2026 ([FS](https://fs.fai.org/download-page/)). Volandoo runs a web admin panel and offers a native app only for retrieve drivers ([Volandoo](https://volandoo.com/event_description)). In organiser work, the phone shows up as a messaging device. The only native move is Gaggle's 2026 run of releases inside its flight-recorder app: submission deadlines and manual rescoring in April, leaderboard management in July, and a live spectator view in August ([Gaggle](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gaggle-paragliding-ultralight/id1556694314)). No published source shows organisers asking to run a whole comp from a phone. The repo itself splits organiser work in two. **Field work suits a phone**: Pilots & tracks is "the organiser's most-used screen during a comp". **Desk work stays on a laptop**: GAP parameters, for example, are "consulted once per competition" ([iOS IA](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-09-22-ios-app-information-architecture.md)). The IA also calls sending a `.xctsk` to an instrument "the native app's best single feature". Stage 6 built it: the app writes the file on the phone, so it works with no signal once the task has been read ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)). Meanwhile, Volandoo already shares tasks by QR with "no extra apps needed" ([Volandoo](https://volandoo.com/event_description)), and a QR code works just as well from a web page or a briefing projector.

## Platform design spent 2025–26 retreating towards legibility

Apple's Liquid Glass (June 2025) made controls "a distinct functional layer that floats above the app" ([Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/102/?time=1098)). NN/g's October 2025 review, an expert critique rather than a user test, found translucent controls hard to see over busy backgrounds, tab bars crowded with smaller touch targets, controls that appear, vanish and move with context, and a back button stripped of its label ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Apple then walked the design back. iOS 26.1 added a "Tinted" option ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2025/10/21/ios-26-1-to-add-optional-opacity-to-liquid-glass/)), which Six Colors judged only a "slight" improvement ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2025/11/soaping-up-liquid-glass-less-transparency-more-contrast/)). The iOS 27 beta replaced the toggle with a slider from clear to tinted, and brought toolbars back to some apps ([TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/news/wwdc26-recap/)). Outlines replaced drop shadows, and small sheets gained a more visible close button ([Design for Native](https://designfornative.com/what-designers-need-to-know-about-ios-27/)). These iOS 27 details come from June 2026 beta reporting; the final release was not checked. Two facts bind GlideComp directly. **Apps built with Xcode 27 can no longer opt out of Liquid Glass** ([Design for Native](https://designfornative.com/what-designers-need-to-know-about-ios-27/)). And on iOS 26 and later, the system draws the background of Expo's native tabs, so `backgroundColor` and `blurEffect` have no effect ([Expo native tabs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/router/native-tabs.md)). The app already uses `NativeTabs` on the SDK 58 line, so it will wear glass whether the team wants it or not. All the team can do is keep the busiest content, maps and 3D tracks, out from under the bar, and test across the whole slider and with Reduce Transparency on.

Glass is also the wrong material for where GlideComp is read. In sunlight, reflected light can swamp the image a screen emits, with measured contrast falling **below 1.75:1** ([USPTO patent 7853288](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7853288)). DisplayMate photographed phones at up to 40,000 lux, found their images progressively overwhelmed, and concluded that anti-reflection coatings help more than extra brightness ([DisplayMate](https://displaymate.com/Smartphone_Brightness_ShootOut_1.htm)). Phones also dim themselves in heat ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/stop-phone-heating-in-hot-weather/)), and forum reports say touch-capable gloves often fail in the cold, which makes small targets hard to hit ([paraglidingforum](https://www.paraglidingforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=65607)). These sources are old or anecdotal, and no rigorous 2024–26 study of outdoor legibility exists. Still, the inference is direct. A design that passes WCAG AA's 4.5:1 indoors has little margin left at a Forbes goal paddock in January. Numbers that matter want opaque backgrounds, heavier weights and large targets. This obligation binds the website and the app alike; it argues for neither.

Google's Material 3 Expressive rests on **46 studies with more than 18,000 participants**. In them, people spotted key elements "up to four times faster", older users found them about as fast as younger ones, and every age group preferred the expressive designs ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Google's own caveats teach more. A playlist redesign that replaced a familiar song list with album art scored *lower* on usability. Removing text labels from email actions reduced usability. And a strong minority preferred calmer designs. The research was run by the vendor and has not been independently replicated. Its lesson matches GlideComp's waypoints rule: big, labelled primary actions help, and turning a results table or pilot list into "expressive" visuals hurts. Expo now makes both platform looks cheap. `@expo/ui` has been stable since SDK 56 and renders SwiftUI and Jetpack Compose with no JavaScript re-implementation ([Expo](https://expo.dev/blog/expo-ui-stable-sdk-56)). Native tabs and toolbars are stable in the SDK 58 beta ([Expo SDK 58](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta)). NN/g draws the same conclusion: when design systems make standard UI cheap, the difference between products moves to content and function ([NN/g State of UX 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)). For GlideComp, that content is explainable scoring.

Meanwhile the web has closed most of the on-screen gap. **Popover** reached Baseline on 2025-01-27, later than first announced, because an iOS bug stopped a tap outside from closing it ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/popover-baseline)). **Same-document view transitions** became Baseline in October 2025 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)), and **cross-document view transitions** work in Chrome 126 and Safari 18.2, opted into with CSS alone ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions/cross-document)). **Anchor positioning and scroll-driven animations** shipped in Safari 26.0 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)), and a **customisable `<select>`** was announced for Safari 27 ([WebKit, WWDC26](https://webkit.org/?p=17967)). Interop 2026 lists mobile testing as an investigation area ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/interop-2026)). For the Astro content pages, cross-document view transitions are a JS-free enhancement that fits the "SEO, not a JS ban" rule. The popover delay is a reminder that anything new needs testing on real iOS Safari.

## Sign-in, onboarding and live surfaces now decide repeat ease

Onboarding research still says to skip the tour. NN/g tested 70 users on four iOS apps. Tutorials made no significant difference to task success (**91% for readers against 94% for skippers**), and readers rated the tasks *harder*: 4.92 against 5.49 on a seven-point scale ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)). The study dates from 2020, outside the window, but it is clean. GlideComp's app already follows it: anyone can read without an account, and sign-in comes "never as an interruption on launch" ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)). Viget's 136-person test found skeleton screens *felt slower* (2.82 s) than a spinner (2.41 s) or a blank screen (2.29 s) ([Viget](https://www.viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens)). That supports keeping GlideComp's live-region `<Loading>` states for layouts a reader has not seen before.

Sign-in is where both clients could make the episodic pilot's return cheaper. The FIDO Passkey Index (October 2025) is self-reported by firms including Amazon, Google, Microsoft and PayPal. It found **passkey sign-ins succeeded 93% of the time against 63% for other methods, and took 8.5 s against 31.2 s** ([FIDO](https://fidoalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FIDO-Passkey-Index-October-2025.pdf)). Apple shipped automatic passkey upgrades in iOS 18. Chrome's "conditional create" quietly makes a passkey straight after an ordinary sign-in ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/passkey-automatic-upgrades)). By inference, the pilot who returns after four months and has forgotten which email they used is exactly the case passkeys fix. An upgrade after the existing email-code sign-in adds no step. Apple's associated-domains file can also carry `webcredentials`, so the app and the site share saved credentials ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/xcode/supporting-associated-domains.md)). Stage 9 already serves that file for Universal Links ([apple-app-site-association](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/functions/.well-known/apple-app-site-association.ts)), but without a `webcredentials` entry.

Glanceable surfaces are the one area of UI where the web cannot follow at all. An iOS Live Activity can stay active for up to eight hours and remain on the Lock Screen for up to twelve ([Apple ActivityKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities.md); [Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797676)). Android's Live Updates are meant for an activity "with a distinct start and end" ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)). A task running from start gate to deadline has exactly that shape. Expo's `expo-widgets` arrived as an alpha in March 2026 ([Expo](https://expo.dev/blog/home-screen-widgets-and-live-activities-in-expo.md)), and the SDK 58 beta adds Android widgets and a Live Activity `staleDate` ([Expo SDK 58](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta)). Gaggle shipped a Live Activity for recording in May 2026 ([Gaggle](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gaggle-paragliding-ultralight/id1556694314)). Evidence of adoption is thin. Just Eat Takeaway reports, about its own app, a **22% lift** in post-order screen views and 42% of users keeping the notification for the whole order ([JET](https://medium.com/justeattakeaway-tech/live-updates-and-progress-notifications-for-android-16-at-jet-b0c87eab17b4)). Meanwhile, most of Google's own apps had not adopted Live Updates ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/amazing-android-feature-live-updates-notifications-3640808/)). The plan lists a widget and a Live Activity only as optional after the beta ([mobile app plan](https://github.com/pokle/glidecomp/blob/f4ce982/docs/2026-10-05-mobile-app-plan.md)). A task countdown drawn from known times needs no server push, so it is the low-risk first step.

AI is the trend that most rewards restraint. NN/g says "2026 is shaping up to be the year of AI fatigue" and expects the winners to treat AI as a tool that "recedes into the background" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)). Where AI does belong in an interface, its 2026 review favours generated buttons and checkboxes over typed follow-ups ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/genui-buttons-and-checkboxes/)). Agents now operate interfaces as users, often through the accessibility tree, so descriptive labels and semantic markup serve people and agents alike ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-agents-as-users/)). The explicit agent hooks are still early. WebMCP is a Chrome 146 Canary preview behind a flag ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/google-chrome-ships-webmcp-in-early-preview-turning-every-website-into-a)), and `expo-app-intents` is alpha in SDK 58 ([Expo SDK 58](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta)). For scores and track submission, which are well-defined repeated tasks, the evidence argues against a chat front end. GlideComp's WCAG 2.2 AA standard and its explainable-scoring rule are already the substrate agents need.

## Conclusion

"Is an app easier?" is the wrong question to measure. On a screen, the website has largely caught up. What the app changes is how many ways a track can fail to arrive between landing and acceptance, and that number falls most on iPhones. The stage 10 beta should therefore be judged by outcomes, not satisfaction: the time from landing to an accepted track, how many tracks the offline queue saved, what share of one comp's field installs the app, and how many of those pilots are still signed in when the next comp starts. The CSP already allows Cloudflare Web Analytics ([_headers](../web/frontend/public/_headers)). If it is switched on, it can show the device and iOS/Android mix before then, and that mix decides how much of the field sits where the app's advantage is largest.

Two implications follow. First, the cheapest gains for the largest group are on the web. A share target and start URL that lead to `/submit` on Android, plus an offline copy of the comp the pilot last opened, would reach the first-comp pilot the app never will. They would also shrink the app's exclusive ground to what iOS withholds from the web: share-sheet import, background upload, dependable push and live surfaces. Second, the app's long-term value depends on its newest and least-tested parts. Pilots lapse between comps, so its lasting audience is organisers and circuit pilots. Stage 7 (organiser tools) and stage 9 (notifications) merged on 24 October, and the beta will show whether they make the app a habit or a once-a-year reinstall. Until then, the website remains the front door and the way back in. Every link a competition sends out will land there, except for the few people who already have the app.
